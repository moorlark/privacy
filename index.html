Nebula

Privacy Policy

Effective 10 October 2026 · applies to the Nebula app for Android (com.nebuladiscovery)

In short

  • Nebula keeps your map on your phone. There is no account, no server of its own, no advertising and no analytics.
  • The developer never receives your location or the places you have explored.
  • Three things do leave your phone: requests for map images (which reveal roughly where you are looking), Google's location service, and Android's own backup if it is switched on. Each is explained below.

1. Who is responsible

Nebula is developed and published by Robin Vavron, who is responsible for the processing described here.

Contact: nebuladiscovery.app@gmail.com

2. What the app stores on your phone

  • Explored places. Nebula divides the world into squares of about 25 by 25 metres. For each square you have been in, it stores the square and the time you first entered it. The individual GPS readings are turned into squares and not kept.
  • Settings, such as the map type, the fog style and the backup folder you chose.
  • Map images that have already been downloaded, so they do not have to be fetched again.

This data stays on your phone until you delete it: in Android's settings under Apps → Nebula → Storage → Clear storage, or by uninstalling the app.

3. Location

Nebula needs your precise location; uncovering the map where you go is all it does. While it records, it runs as a foreground service and shows a notification the whole time.

If you choose to allow location access “all the time”, Nebula uses it for one thing only: to start recording again by itself after the phone restarts or the app is updated. Without it, the app asks you to tap a notification instead. Everything else works the same.

Your position is read through the fused location provider of Google Play services. Depending on your phone's “Google Location Accuracy” setting, Google may collect location data for its own purposes; this is governed by Google's Privacy Policy and can be changed in Android's location settings.

4. Map images

To draw the map, the app downloads map images (“tiles”) from one of two services, depending on the map type you pick:

  • Standard map: the tile servers of the OpenStreetMap Foundation, delivered through a worldwide network of cache servers.
  • Satellite map: Esri World Imagery, operated by Esri in the United States. Esri is certified under the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework; see Esri's Privacy Statement.

Every request contains your IP address and which part of the map is shown. Since you are usually looking at the map around you, a service could infer your approximate location from this. Your GPS position itself is not sent. Requests also name the app, its version and the contact address above, as OpenStreetMap's usage rules ask. What the services log and for how long is set out in their own policies.

Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR – the map cannot be shown without these requests.

5. Backups

Backups to a folder you choose

Nebula can save backup files to a folder you pick. They contain your explored squares with the time each was first entered. They stay wherever you put them; if that folder is synchronised with a cloud service, that service's terms apply.

Android backup

Android can back up the app's data – including your explored places, but not the downloaded map images – to your Google account, so it can be restored on a new phone. This is Android's backup, run by Google under Google's Privacy Policy; the developer has no access to it. You can switch it off in Android's settings under Google → Backup.

6. What the developer receives

  • From the app: nothing. The app has no connection to the developer.
  • Emails you send. If you write to the contact address, your email and address are used only to answer you and are deleted once the conversation is over, unless the law requires otherwise. Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR – the legitimate interest in answering you.
  • Google Play statistics. Google Play gives developers aggregated figures such as installs, and crash reports from users who allowed their phone to share them. These do not identify you to the developer and are governed by Google's policies.

Nebula contains no advertising, no analytics and no tracking, and no data is sold.

7. Children

Nebula is not directed at children under 13.

8. Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to access, rectification, erasure, restriction of processing, data portability and to object, and the right to lodge a complaint with a data protection supervisory authority, in particular in the country where you live. Since the developer holds no data about you other than emails you may have sent, such requests can only concern those; the data on your phone is entirely under your control (see section 2). To exercise your rights, write to the contact address.
